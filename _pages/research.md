My research is primarily focused on studying tidal interactions between astrophysical objects, and the impact of tidal deformation on fluid bodies. You can read more about these topics by following the links to my papers in the [Publications](/publications/) page

# Tidal Disruption Events

A star that ventures too close to a supermassive black hole ($M_\bullet \gtrsim 10^5 M_\odot$) can be ripped apart by tides -- in a tidal disruption event (TDE). A fraction of the stellar debris is bound to the black hole and accretes onto it, providing a short supply of fuel that illuminates the otherwise quiescent supermassive black hole. TDEs enable us to probe the dynamics of stars near galactic nuclei, and the population of supermassive black holes in our Universe.

In the last decade, time-domain surveys have uncovered a growing population of TDE-like transient flares that re-brighten months to years after their original discovery. These events have been shown to arise from the repeated partial tidal disruption of stars on bound orbits, in a repeating partial tidal disruption event. By virtue of the repeating nature of the flares they emit, these systems enable us to probe the interiors of stars, stellar rotation and the formation channels for these systems, in addition to the properties of the supermassive black hole. 


# Binary Neutron Star Mergers

Two neutron stars orbiting each other inspiral into one-another due to the emission of gravitational waves. During this gravitational wave driven inspiral phase, the tidal interaction between the two neutron stars distorts each star, which dissipates orbital energy and accelerates the inspiral. The tidal interaction leaves its imprint on the gravitational waveform of the inspiral/merger, which is parameterized in terms of the tidal deformability of each neutron star. Measuring the tidal deformability enables us to probe the interior composition of neutron stars, and study the properties of strongly interacting nuclear matter.
